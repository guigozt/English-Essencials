📚 Using 's and Whose
1️⃣ Possessive 's

Use 's to show:

Family relationships
Possession

Examples:

John's father
Sarah's phone
Maria's house
The teacher's book
2️⃣ Possessive s'

If the noun is a regular plural ending in s, add only an apostrophe.

Examples:

My parents' house.
The students' books.
The teachers' room.

📌 Compare

| Singular            | Plural              |
| ------------------- | ------------------- |
| The girl's book.    | The girls' books.   |
| The teacher's desk. | The teachers' room. |


3️⃣ Whose

Use whose to ask who owns something.

Examples:

Whose book is this?
Whose phone is that?
Whose keys are these?
Whose bag is it?

Answers:

It's John's.
It's Maria's.
They're my parents'.
⚠️ Whose × Who's

These words sound the same (homophones), but they have different meanings.

| Word      | Meaning          | Example              |
| --------- | ---------------- | -------------------- |
| **Who's** | Who is / Who has | Who's your teacher?  |
| **Whose** | De quem          | Whose phone is this? |
